# Storytelling & Content Craft — the positive playbook

> **What this is:** the _positive_ craft companion to `VOICE_RUBRIC.md` — the reusable storytelling and content frameworks that make a post/video **good**, distilled and filtered for diBoaS. The rubric and the checklists say what's _forbidden_; this says what's _worth doing_.
> **Derives from and defers to:** `docs/full-view/BRAND_POSITIONING.md` §Voice & Tone → The Writing System. On any conflict, the positioning document and the veto gates win.
> **How it was built:** mined 2026-07-13 from 11 reference transcripts in `docs/socials/storytelling/`, every item filtered through the three-nevers and the no-dark-patterns rule. The tactics those sources push that diBoaS **rejects** are collected in §9 and cross-referenced to the gates.
> **How to use it — two modes:** _BUILD_ — structure copy/asset with §§1–8 before drafting, then score with the Voice Rubric (Q1–Q4) and run the anti-slop / asset-compliance checklists. _AUDIT_ — score already-built content with the **craft scorecard in §10** (C1–C6) alongside the Voice Rubric, and write craft findings in the F-format. This is **advisory, not a gate**: a weak craft score means "make it better," never a merge block — the three nevers and the veto rows remain the only veto authority. Wired into `UX_GOVERNANCE_USAGE.md` Mode 1 (build) and Mode 2 (audit).
> **The one rule that governs all of it:** _if a technique requires manufacturing a feeling the truth didn't earn, it's out._

## 1. The per-asset story spine — decide this before writing anything

- **The 5-line method:** Situation → Desire → Conflict → Change → Result. Nail the emotional core in five lines before any detail (gear, transitions, format). Scales cleanly 5→10→20 lines without changing the core. Every post/video gets one.
- **Logline test:** one sentence — _[the reader] wants [X] but [obstacle]_. If you can't write it, the piece has no spine.
- **The Three C's of emotion:** Content + Character + Circumstance firing at once. For diBoaS: **Adelaide** (character) + her money moment (content) + the system that failed her (circumstance).
- **The Four Pillars of a piece:** grab attention → make them feel → teach one real thing → leave them wanting more. A fast structural check for any post or video.
- **Character profile** (write Adelaide, BAR, and the reader like screenplay characters): what they **want**, what they **need**, their **nemesis** (the system / opacity), their **dream**. This depth is what makes the feeling land.
- **The reader is the hero; diBoaS is the ally.** The brand never plays the hero — it is the guide that hands the hero the door she never got. This is the narrative form of "never selling."

## 2. Structure the middle — "But / Therefore," never "and then"

Join every beat with **but** or **therefore**, never "and then." "And then" piles on detail and loses people; "but/therefore" creates the open loops (conflict) and closes them (context) that keep attention. Write the beats out and check the connector. Applies to scripts _and_ multi-line captions.

## 3. Hooks — honest, never clickbait

- **Context in the first line.** Say what the piece is about; the brain reads top-down (context → how → example → lesson). No opaque "wait till you see this."
- **Three-surface alignment.** The visual, the on-screen text, and the spoken/first line all say the **same** thing. (This is also an asset-gate check.)
- **Visual hook beats audio.** Show while you tell — a real image on screen confirming the subject, not just a talking mouth.
- **"Call them by their name."** Name the reader's real fear or moment so precisely they recognise themselves ("She saved with care. The system paid her back with silence."). Recognition, not selling — the single most on-brand hook we have.
- **Belief → truthful reveal (contrast).** "Most think X; here's what's actually true." Only ever a _true_ reframe, never a promise.
- **Honest open loops / questions.** A question the piece genuinely answers. Never widen the gap to withhold, never a cliffhanger that doesn't pay off.
- **For educational pieces (Learn / YouTube), the 3P intro:** who is speaking (proof) → the path you'll walk → what you'll **understand** by the end. The "promise" is always understanding — **never** a money outcome (that would breach never-promising).

## 4. Make them feel it — Draper, operationalised

- **Zoom into the moment; don't summarise.** Be in the trenches, not at helicopter level. Five tools: **Location · Action · Thought · Emotion · Dialogue.**
- **Show emotion on the body — never name it.** "He leaned back and exhaled" > "he was relieved." "Her hand paused over the envelope" > "she was worried."
- **One-of-one lived narrative.** "Someone I love hit this → here's what happened → take it or leave it." This form _is_ "never advising" — it shares an experience instead of issuing a "you should."
- **Emotion from truth and character only** — never from manufactured stakes or fake urgency.

## 5. Rhythm & clarity — Adelaide, operationalised

- **Vary sentence length; one sentence per line.** Look down the left edge — a "jagged edge" means varied rhythm; a straight edge means monotony (churn). (Reinforces anti-slop Part 2 "uniform sentence length.")
- **One question per line.** Each line should open exactly one question the next line answers. A script/thread validator.
- **Clarity is the KPI, not word count.** Confusion → churn. Cut fluff; add words _only_ if they add clarity. Active voice.
- **Write to one friend.** Conversational tone dissolves the "am I being sold to?" guard — which is the point, because we aren't selling.
- **Know what to leave out** (Twain). Withhold detail to invite the lean-in.

## 6. Payoff & honesty

- **Bait, then give — never bait-and-switch.** Close every loop you open. A hook that doesn't pay off is a broken promise.
- **Native-embed any CTA** into the narrative; never a jarring "act now" interrupt.
- **Write the ending first** ("the last dab"); make the last line memorable and, for looping short-form, a setup for the first.

## 7. Campaign structure — series & format

- **Hook + one Goal per post** (one intended action: share / save / reply). Never many.
- **~4 content pillars**; rotate _topics_ faster than _format_. Stay within one topic lane; widen only into subtopics.
- **A recognisable, repeatable format** = "tuning in" (same template/aesthetic across LinkedIn / X / Instagram).
- **Numbered series** ("Day X of 30") for bingeability and completion pull — fits the 30-day arc.
- **Lego-brick decomposition:** topic · angle · hook (visual/text/spoken) · structure · format · key visuals · audio. Hold the world-class bricks constant; improve one at a time; differentiate on a single brick.
- **The subway-map planner** (for any campaign): Why (anchor) → Destinations (≤3 goals/season) → Signals → Audience-per-line → Formats (stops) → Timetable. Work _backwards_: goal → signal → content.
- **Niche-first.** One persona per platform. "Speak to everyone = speak to no one." Founder/team voice filters for the right audience.
- **Story lens:** our unique angle on a common topic — the calm, honest, anti-hype money story. It's how we're a category of one.
- **Faces over logos.** A real person (BAR, a team member, a relatable character) builds trust faster than a brand mark — and filters for the right audience.
- **Ship the simplest whole thing** (the "skateboard," not half a car): one clear message per asset, never a feature dump.
- **For video, diagnose the retention graph** (where people drop off) and **re-hook at each segment transition** — earn attention again honestly, never with a manufactured cliffhanger.

## 8. Measurement — anti-vanity (validates the brand's existing stance)

- **Define the success metric before publishing;** ignore off-goal spikes (views, followers).
- **Measure conversation quality** — engaged replies, shares → DMs, waitlist _quality_ — not raw reach.
- **"Memorable minutes over forgettable seconds."** Virality is a sugar rush, not a goal.
- **Growth = consistency × experimentation.** Interrogate the failures, not the wins. Judge the channel, not one post; treat a comment as signal only when the sentiment repeats.

## 9. ⚠️ Tactics these sources push that diBoaS REJECTS

Every source leaned on growth tactics that hit our veto list. They are collected here so they're recognised and refused, not absorbed. Each maps to an existing gate — **no new veto gate is needed; these are already forbidden.**

| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Manufactured urgency, countdowns, "post in 24h", "going fast"               | never selling    | anti-slop Part 3 **row 10**           |
| Scarcity / "first 50 only" / FOMO "desire loop"                             | never selling    | Part 3 **row 10**                     |
| Volume/herd/follower-count as credibility; EMV seeding at scale             | herd proof       | Part 3 **row 11**; asset-gate         |
| Clickbait, curiosity-gap _widening_, shock titles, "negative/inverse" hooks | dishonest        | Voice Rubric **Q3**; anti-slop Part 2 |
| Rage-bait / "make them fight in the comments" / engagement-bait             | manipulation     | Gate 4 (no dark patterns)             |
| Shock-for-views / "sensationalist / shock score" to juice reach             | anti-hype breach | positioning + Q1 (truth)              |
| Fabricated personas, undisclosed AI influencers, fake collabs/proof         | fabrication      | asset-gate **A1**; Q3                 |
| "Portfolio feedback / not advice but…" , "you should"                       | never advising   | Voice Rubric **Q3**                   |
| Guaranteed-outcome framing ("100k in 18 months")                            | never promising  | Voice Rubric **Q3**                   |
| Bait-and-switch / hooks that never pay off                                  | dishonest        | §6 above + Gate 1 (truth)             |
| Dopamine-engineering / "hypnotise / lull into a delusional state"           | manipulation     | Gate 4                                |

**The test again:** a technique that manufactures a feeling the truth didn't earn — urgency, scarcity, outrage, a promise, a withheld payoff — is out, regardless of measured lift. The competitor using it on someone's savings is exactly what makes diBoaS the adult in the room.

## 10. The craft scorecard — the audit + build lens

The distilled diagnostic form of §§1–8. Use it two ways: in BUILD, as a pre-draft checklist ("have I got all six?"); in AUDIT, score each surface **PASS / PARTIAL / MISS** with a one-line pointer. It answers _"is this any good?"_, which the veto gates never ask. **It is advisory** — a MISS is a craft finding (enrich / revise), never a blocker. Blocking stays with the three nevers (Voice Rubric Q3) and the anti-slop veto rows 10–17.

| #      | Craft signal                  | PASS looks like                                                                                                | Common MISS                                                                  | Source § |
| ------ | ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| **C1** | **Story spine**               | one clear spine — the **reader is the hero**, diBoaS is the ally; a want, a turn, a resolution                 | a feature list or a value-prop sandwich; the brand is the hero               | §1       |
| **C2** | **But / Therefore**           | the middle turns on tension → consequence ("…but… therefore…")                                                 | "and then… and then…" — flat sequence, no stakes                             | §2       |
| **C3** | **Honest hook**               | opens on a real, context-first, felt moment; earns the read without a curiosity gap                            | clickbait, manufactured mystery, or a cold abstract claim as the opener      | §3       |
| **C4** | **Show, don't name** (Draper) | dramatises the feeling in one concrete moment (the lease you'd walk through, the 10 minutes before a deadline) | names the emotion/benefit abstractly ("feel confident", "peace of mind")     | §4       |
| **C5** | **Earned payoff**             | the ending pays off the hook _truthfully_ — the feeling was earned by the truth, not manufactured              | bait-and-switch; a payoff the body didn't earn; a CTA bolted on              | §6       |
| **C6** | **Series & human-first**      | fits a coherent series/format; leads with **faces & moments over logos/charts**                                | one-off with no series logic; logo/chart/analytics-theater as the lead image | §§5,7    |

**Reading the score:** six PASS = the reference bar (name it so nobody "fixes" it). Any MISS or cluster of PARTIALs = a craft finding, ranked enrich / revise / leave. Score every locale in its own register (a line can PASS in EN and be flat in pt-BR). A craft finding never overrides a veto — if Q3 or a veto row also fails, that routing wins.
